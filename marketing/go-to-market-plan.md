# Orbit go-to-market plan

Goal: $10,000 a month in revenue, which is about 1,667 paying students at $6 a month.
Rules that still apply: no dashes in copy, only real numbers, nothing that implies Orbit sends messages for you.

## 1. The honest math (read this first)

Your cost to serve a paying student is about $1.79 a month, so each monthly payer leaves about **$4.25 a month**.
A yearly payer ($48) leaves about **$30 over the year** (AI $14.76, payments $2.03, hosting about $0.50).

What a paying student costs in ads depends on two numbers: cost per trial student, and how many trials turn into
payers. Published benchmarks for the second one disagree a lot. For trials with no credit card, one analysis
(ChartMogul, via Userpilot) puts the median at **4 to 6%** and the top quarter at **10 to 15%**; other sources
say 18%. Plan on the low end until your own data says otherwise.

Cost per paying student, by cost per trial and conversion:

| Cost per trial | 5% convert | 10% convert | 18% convert |
|---|---|---|---|
| $5 | $100 | $50 | $28 |
| $10 | $200 | $100 | $56 |
| $15 | $300 | $150 | $83 |

At $4.25 a month of profit per monthly payer, a $100 customer takes about two years to pay back. A $28 customer
takes about seven months. A yearly payer at $28 pays back inside the first year.

What that means:
- **Paid ads alone cannot build this business at $6 a month.** They are for testing and for scaling what already works.
- **Free channels do the real work:** campus, student organizations, founder content, and students telling students.
- **Push the yearly plan.** It brings in $48 up front, cuts payment fees from 8.6% to 4.2%, and covers a student
  who only recruits for a semester.
- With $1,500, you will learn which message, which channel and which conversion rate you really have. That is
  worth more than the 5 to 45 paying students it might buy.

## 2. The strategy in one sentence

Win one campus at a time through the people students already trust (organizations, professors, career center),
make every student you win bring two friends, and spend ad money only on messages and channels that have already
proven they work.

## 3. Timing: recruiting season is now

Per university career guides, Big Four internship applications typically open in late August and close in late
September, with the main recruiting period running September through March. Some firms interview sophomores for
spring externships early in the year. Firms differ, so check each firm's schedule.

- **October to November (now):** juniors are mid-application and sophomores are researching. Pitch: "Who do you know at the firm you're applying to?" and "Follow up after every coffee chat."
- **January to March:** spring recruiting and sophomore externships. Pitch: "Prep for the interview and the coffee chat."
- **April to August:** quiet. Pitch the yearly plan and "get ahead before fall."
Run your hardest campus push in the next 6 weeks and again in January.

## 4. Channels, in order (cheapest and most trusted first)

**1. Campus and organizations (free, highest trust)**
- Targets at Mississippi State first: Beta Alpha Psi, the accounting society, finance and investment clubs, the career center, the Entrepreneurship Center, 3 to 5 accounting and finance professors.
- The ask: a 3 minute demo at a meeting, or a link in a class announcement. Bring a QR code to buildyourorbit.com.
- Why it works: one yes reaches 30 to 100 students with the trust of someone they know.
- Then copy it: repeat at 5 other schools you can reach (friends there, club presidents on LinkedIn).

**2. Founder content (free, compounds)**
- LinkedIn: 3 posts a week for 6 weeks. Mix: a recruiting tip, a clip from the ad videos, one honest build update.
- Instagram and TikTok: post the 20 video ads as normal Reels and shorts, plus 2 selfie videos a week ("what I learned from a coffee chat").
- Reply to every comment within the hour. Comment on posts from accounting and finance students and professionals.
- Reddit, GroupMe and Discord: answer questions in r/Big4 and r/accounting without selling. One honest mention when it fits, following each community's rules.

**3. Student-to-student loop (cheap, scales)**
- In the app: "Invite 2 friends and you each get a free month of Pro." Cost per free month is about $1.23 in AI, far cheaper than an ad.
- Campus leads: pick 1 student at each of 5 other schools. Free Pro for life, plus a small bonus per signup that they pass through (test the amount).

**4. Paid ads (test, then scale)**
See section 6. Instagram Reels and TikTok only. Skip LinkedIn ads: cost per click is roughly $5 to $12 against $0.50 to $2 on Instagram and TikTok, which does not fit a $6 product.

## 6. The ad plan for the $1,500

Targeting: age 18 to 24, plus a radius around the campuses you can reach. Meta has been removing school and
major targeting, so confirm in Ads Manager what is still offered (Demographics, then Education) and otherwise
rely on location, age and creative that names accounting and finance. After about 100 signups, build a lookalike
audience from them. Send traffic to buildyourorbit.com with the UTM tags in `ad-copy-variants.md`.

Stages and budget:

| Stage | What | Spend |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Screen | 10 creatives, one per angle, $15 each. Judge on click cost and click rate only | $150 |
| 2. Prove | The best 4, $45 each. Judge on cost per signup | $180 |
| 3. Scale | Put the winner on Instagram and TikTok | $700 |
| 4. Campus | The winner aimed at a radius around 5 target schools | $300 |
| Reserve | Keep for what the data shows | $170 |

Decision rules (your earlier $15 rule is too noisy because $15 is only about 3 trials, so I tightened it):
- After $15: kill anything with a cost per click above about $2 or almost no clicks.
- After $45: kill anything with zero signups. Scale anything under $5 a signup.
- Track the next step too. A cheap signup who never imports LinkedIn is worthless, so judge ads on **signup then import**.

Creatives to start with: `AdFollowUp`, `AdPromise`, `AdWhoYouKnow` (strongest problems), plus `AdSpreadsheet` and
`AdFounder` (a different tone). Static: the three special images (comparison, playbook, checklist) as feed ads.

## 7. What to say to each audience

- **Students:** "You said you'd send your resume. Did you?" and "Applying to a firm? Check who you already know there."
- **Org presidents:** "A free tool for your members. 3 minutes at your next meeting. I'll share what I learn." Offer the club a free month for every member.
- **Professors and career center:** "Student built, free for 14 days, never sends messages for the student, data private to the student." Ask for a link in a class announcement or the career center newsletter.

## 8. What to build in the app (this is where I can help most)

You cannot run ads blind. These are the tools to add, in order:
1. **Analytics and ad pixels:** Vercel Analytics, a Meta pixel and a TikTok pixel, with events for signup, LinkedIn import, return in week 2, and paid.
2. **Source tracking:** save the UTM source on each new account, so every signup says which ad or post it came from.
3. **A monthly AI spend cap per paying student** (about $3), so no one can cost more than they pay.
4. **Invite-a-friend with a referral code** and the free-month reward.
5. **Yearly plan as the default** in checkout, with the monthly plan one click away.
6. **A short onboarding that gets the LinkedIn export imported fast**, since import is what turns a signup into a user.

## 9. What to measure each week

| Number | Why |
|---|---|
| Landing visitors to signups | Is the page working? |
| Signups to LinkedIn import within 48 hours | Does a student reach value? |
| Students who return in week 2 | The real signal of a habit |
| Trial to paid | Compare to the 4 to 6% planning number |
| Cost per signup by source | Where to put money |
| Yearly share of payers | Cash and margin |
